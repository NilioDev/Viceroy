# Viceroy

**Play the 1994 DOS classic *Sid Meier's Colonization* on today's computers,
phones and tablets, without an emulator.**

Viceroy is a free, open-source, from-scratch engine for the original game.
Bring your own copy of the DOS version: Viceroy reads its files and plays the
game exactly as the original does, with the same rules, screens and
savegames, scaled sharply to any screen and with gamepad, mouse, keyboard and
touch controls.

> **Status: first playable steps.** Start a new game on the map of America
> (difficulty, nation and name, the king's audience and the departure, as
> in the original) or load one of your savegames, move your
> units over the original map (or send them to a colony with Go To, along
> the original's routes), board ships and make landfall, plow fields,
> clear forests and build roads with your pioneers, found colonies, look inside your colonies, end turns and save. The colony screen
> shows its buildings, people, what they produce and the warehouse; give
> colonists other jobs with the jobs menu, and choose what the colony builds
> on its Make panel or pay to finish it there; the musket button lists the
> units in the colony; click one to move it to the front, clear its orders,
> sentry or fortify it. At the end of the turn colonies
> store what they make, breed horses from spare food, grow and finish
> the buildings, wagon trains, artillery and ships under way, and your
> advisors report full warehouses (with Tutorial Hints on, the first time
> comes with a hint on shipping the cargo home), waste, food running low and workshops
> out of raw goods; a colony that stays without food loses colonists to
> hunger. Ships sail to Europe from
> the high seas, sell and buy goods there (prices move as you trade), take colonists from the docks and
> sail back. In Europe you can recruit colonists, buy artillery and ships,
> and have specialists trained. Crosses made in your colonies and churches
> bring colonists over from the docks for free. Liberty bells win colonists
> for the Sons of Liberty: at 50% and 100% membership a colony works
> better, and too many tories slow it down. Soldiers, dragoons and
> artillery can attack the other nations' units and colonies on the map,
> and take a colony with its share of the owner's gold: you are asked
> before breaking a peace treaty, the Combat Analysis option shows the
> odds, the fight is rolled the way the original rolls it (with its help
> on the easy levels), the loser is demoted, damaged or captured, and a
> winning soldier can harden to a veteran. Warships fight other ships: the
> weaker one may evade, and a beaten ship sinks or limps to a drydock or
> Europe for repairs. Colonists can walk into a native settlement and
> live among the natives to learn the skill it teaches, and scouts can
> speak with the chief for guides, tales of the land, a gift or an
> early death, as in the original. The liberty bells of your colonies
> fill the Continental Congress: you choose the next founding father from
> one candidate of each kind, and when enough bells have rung he joins and
> appears in the hall of congress, with what he brings at once (a frigate
> from John Paul Jones, stockades from La Salle, calm tribes from
> Pocahontas and so on), and Jefferson, Paine, Penn and Hudson make your
> colonies ring more bells, preach more and trap more furs from then on, as
> printing presses and newspapers do. Magellan's ships sail further,
> Drake's privateers fight harder, Bolivar raises the Sons of Liberty,
> Adam Smith and Peter Stuyvesant open factories and the custom house to
> your build lists, and with Paul Revere your colonists take up muskets to
> defend a colony. The Continental Congress report (F3) shows the next
> session, the bells toward it, the rebels and tories, the King's forces
> and the fathers in congress. The King raises your taxes as he does in the
> original; you may refuse a rise with a party that boycotts the good in
> Europe until you pay the back taxes. He builds up his army, tells you
> when your colonists' rebel sentiment rises or falls, offers to carry your
> treasure home for the Crown's share, and now and then a European king
> offers you mercenaries. When your men meet another European nation, its
> leader comes to talk: offering a treaty, demanding tribute, goods or the
> withdrawal of your troops, declaring war, or, at peace, hearing your
> requests for withdrawal, gifts or an alliance against a third party.
> The computer nations make peace and war among themselves, and you hear
> of it. A scout at another nation's colony can meet its mayor or try to
> spy on the colony; with Jan de Witt your wagon trains and ships can
> trade with other nations' colonies. The King may drag you into his wars in Europe, and
> when your rebels reach half, the War of the Spanish Succession hands the
> weakest nation's colonies to another. With a majority of rebels you can
> declare independence from the Game menu: the other nations leave, your
> ships in Europe are seized, your veterans join the Continental Army,
> mercenaries are offered, and enough liberty bells bring a foreign power
> and its force to your side. The King's men-of-war land his army next to
> your colonies, and when he has none left to send, the Tories rise. The
> war is won when his army is beaten and your colonies are free, and lost
> with your ports, your colonies or most of your people; you are warned
> before. How his army fights on is still to come. Without a war the
> Viceroy retires in 1800 (in 1850 during it), and the other nations' kings
> may let their colonies go. Losing all your colonies after 1600 ends the
> game. The other nations elect their own
> fathers as the original's do. A tribe you meet for the first time
> offers you peace; refusing it angers the tribe. Missionaries can found missions,
> which the tribe takes well or badly as in the original, pay a tribe to
> go to war against another nation, or denounce another nation's mission
> to take it over. A furious tribe with a treaty may burn your missions.
> Native settlements grow, raise new braves, and calm down or grow angry
> from missions and nearby colonies each turn, as in the original. Their
> braves roam, fortify, take up muskets and horses at home and head for
> nearby colonies the way the original's do. Angry braves attack: they
> ambush units in the open and take their muskets and horses, kill the
> people of an undefended colony or burn it with its last colonist, and a
> raiding party that loses at a colony may still steal goods and gold,
> burn a building or damage a ship in the harbour. Braves also visit your
> colonies and wagon trains: a hungry tribe begs for food, a friendly one
> brings food, goods or converts from your mission, and an unfriendly one
> demands goods as reparations, which you may hand over or refuse. The
> other European nations have begun to play: each takes its turn in the
> original's order and buys in Europe as the original's computer players
> do (ships when it is short of them, recruits, soldiers, dragoons,
> pioneers, artillery and the goods its colonies ask for), helped by the
> Crown's gold. Their units act on the map as the original's do: they
> look for sites and found colonies, join colonies, guard them, build
> roads, explore, visit villages, and their ships carry people and goods
> between Europe and their colonies. When the odds are good they attack
> the units and colonies of nations they have no peace with, and the
> villages of tribes they are at war with. Their colonies watch for
> threats, call for guards, arm colonists as soldiers, take in newcomers,
> ask for the goods they lack, put their colonists to work, choose what
> to build, teach in their schools, train farmers and fishermen, and plow,
> clear and lay roads on their own land. Wagon trains and ships can trade with the
> natives: sell, haggle, give and buy at the original's prices. Soldiers,
> scouts and other armed units can demand tribute, which a village pays
> in goods for your nearest colony when your forces nearby impress it, and
> the chief's face shows how angry the tribe is. They can also attack a
> village and burn it, for treasure, converts and the tribe's anger. Fish,
> ore and the other resources show on the map where the original puts them
> and add to what your colonists make there. Lost city rumours show on the
> map too, and exploring one brings what it brings in the original: a
> Fountain of Youth, a city of gold, ruins, burial mounds, a friendly
> tribe's gift, survivors of a lost colony, angry shrines, nothing, or an
> expedition that vanishes. Scouts and Hernando de Soto do better.
>
> The music plays during a game: the original's own tunes, read from its
> sound driver for OPL3 cards (Sound Blaster Pro and 16) and played on an
> emulated chip, with the recorded drums of a card that plays samples. As
> in the original a new tune starts whenever one ends, and events change the
> mood of the music as they do there (a famine, a sunk ship, meeting a tribe,
> a lost city). Fights, raids, sinking ships and new missions have the
> original's sound effects. Sound Options in the Game menu turn background
> music, event music and sound effects on or off. Pick Music chooses the
> next tune yourself.

Viceroy is not affiliated with or endorsed by the makers of the original
game. It contains none of the original game's files: you need your own copy.

## Planned

- 🎯 A one-to-one copy of the DOS version: same rules, same look, same
  computer players.
- 🖥️ Windows, macOS, Linux and Android from one codebase.
- 🎮 Full gamepad support: move units with the stick or D-pad, jump between
  units and colonies with the shoulder buttons, and a cursor that snaps to
  buildings, colonists and cargo in the colony and Europe screens. Every
  button can be changed.
- 💾 Your old savegames keep working.

## Try it

There are no releases yet. Every build on `main` makes test packages: open
the [latest CI run on main](https://github.com/NilioDev/Viceroy/actions/workflows/ci.yml?query=branch%3Amain)
and download one at the bottom of the page.

- 🐧 **Linux:** `Viceroy-Linux-AppImage`. Unpack the zip, make the
  `.AppImage` executable (right-click, *Properties*, *Allow executing file as
  program*, or `chmod +x`) and double-click it.
- 🪟 **Windows:** `Viceroy-Windows`, then start `Viceroy.exe`.
- 🍎 **macOS:** `Viceroy-macOS`.

## Controls

When the game files are in place, Viceroy plays the original's opening and
then shows its main menu. **Start a Game in AMERICA** and **LOAD Game** work
there; the New World maps and the customizing come later. A new game is
saved in the first free slot, or over the savegame saved longest ago.

The mouse pointer is the game's own arrow, drawn in the picture as the
original does; it hides while you play with a gamepad.

**Debug menu.** F12, or Back and Start together on a gamepad, opens a menu
for testing that is not part of the original game: reveal the map, add gold,
give units their moves back, end the turn, or jump to your next colony. On a
colony screen it adds goods, a colonist, a level to every building or Sons of
Liberty. Nothing changes unless you pick an entry.

Saving writes over the savegame you loaded, in Viceroy's library (see
below). Your original files are never changed.

## Add your game

You hand Viceroy your own copy of the DOS game **once**. On first start it
asks for it:

- drop the game folder (the one with `VICEROY.EXE`) or the `.zip` it came in
  onto the window, or
- press **Enter** or the gamepad's **A** button to choose the folder, or
  **Z** or **Y** to choose the zip file.

Viceroy checks the files, copies them into its own library and uses them from
then on, so you can delete or move the original afterwards. The savegames that
came with the game are copied too; your own saves are never overwritten. The
game may sit in a subfolder of the folder or zip, and Mac leftovers such as
`__MACOSX` are ignored. To import again, for example from another copy, just
drop it onto the window.

The library is in `~/.local/share/Viceroy/Viceroy/library` on Linux,
`%APPDATA%\Viceroy\Viceroy\library` on Windows and
`~/Library/Application Support/Viceroy/Viceroy/library` on macOS.

If there is a `gamedata` folder with the game next to the program, next to the
`.AppImage` file or in the current folder, Viceroy imports it by itself. You
can also give a folder or zip when starting it: `./Viceroy path/to/col.zip`.
